- `crates/exl3/src/lib.rs`, `crates/exl3/src/checkpoint.rs`: port of `src/tensorfold/cuda/exl3/format.py` and the
  bit arithmetic in `docs/recipes/exl3.md`.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/exl3_linear.cu`, `crates/cuda/csrc/exl3_linear_decode.cuh`: ports of `src/tensorfold/cuda/exl3/linear.cu`
  and `decode.cuh` (the tile decode into `mma.m16n8k16` fragments, the fixed-order decode kernel with its (K, N) split
  plan, the input rotation and unpack kernels), with the ATen wrappers replaced by C-ABI launchers, the split-K

  its API follows `linear.py`.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/moe_experts.cu`, `crates/cuda/src/moe.rs`: the MoE expert pipeline follows TensorFold's grouped EXL3 expert GEMV
  (`src/tensorfold/cuda/exl3/experts_grouped.cuh`, `experts.cu`, `experts.py`: grouping of (row, slot) pairs by expert, per-pair input
  rotation, the 16-row mma tile per expert, fixed-order warp and split sums, the down epilogue and slot combine), rewritten with C-ABI
  launchers over REXL3's own tile decode, a per-layer compile-time width, the oracle's clamped SwiGLU, a slot-order fmaf combine with the
  shared expert added last, and a shape-only plan. `crates/cuda/csrc/moe_router.cu` is original code that reproduces ExLlamaV3's one-row
  router GEMV arithmetic (see below). `crates/cuda/tests/moe.rs` and the `tools/*moe*.py` scripts are REXL3's own.
- `testdata/fixtures/*.json` are outputs of TensorFold's Python on seeded inputs, generated by `tools/gen_fixtures.py`.
  The script loads TensorFold at run time and copies no code.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/gdn.cu`: port of `src/tensorfold/cuda/kernels/gdn.cu` (the replay `step()`, the chain window kernel and
  the replay kernel) and `src/tensorfold/cuda/kernels/gdn_prefill.cu` (the prefill `chain_kernel`); the conv, silu, l2norm
  and gating prep is a CUDA rewrite of the Triton `_gdn_pre` in `src/tensorfold/families/qwen3_5/cuda/glue.py`. `crates/cuda/src/gdn.rs` and
  `crates/cuda/tests/gdn.rs` are REXL3's own wrappers and tests for it.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/attention.cu`, `crates/cuda/src/attention.rs`: paged KV cache and attention. The prefill kernel is
  a CUDA port of `src/tensorfold/cuda/kernels/prefill_attention.cu` (staging, swizzle, mma tile structure; ATen wrapper and
  the Triton-matching arithmetic removed), the mma / ldmatrix / cp.async helpers come from
  `src/tensorfold/cuda/kernels/qmm_frag.cuh`, and the decode kernels (absolute 512-key chunks, per-chunk fp32
  online-softmax partials, in-order merge) are a CUDA rewrite of the Triton kernels in
  `src/tensorfold/cuda/kernels/attention.py`.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/mla.cu`, `crates/cuda/src/mla.rs`, `crates/cuda/tests/mla.rs`: GLM-5.3-Flash MLA (dense regime, and a sparse gather over the DSA selection). A CUDA rewrite of the
  structure of `src/tensorfold/families/glm5_next/cuda/latent.py` (absolute key-chunk programs with fp32 partials merged in chunk order,
  absorb and expand as per-row fixed-order sums over the latent); the mma, ldmatrix and cp.async helpers and the log2-domain fold come from
  the REXL3 GQA decode kernel above. The numerical oracle is the ExLlamaV3 fork's `MLAttention` (MIT), used for comparison only.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/glue.cu`: CUDA rewrite of the kernels in `src/tensorfold/families/qwen3_5/cuda/glue.py` (Triton:
  fused add + RMSNorm, attention prep, gate multiply, gated norm, SwiGLU, embedding gather) and the fixed-order
  one-warp-per-output reduction of `src/tensorfold/families/qwen3_5/cuda/b16.cu` (small fp16-weight linear). The
  top-k stage is original (TensorFold's `src/tensorfold/cuda/sampling.py` only calls `torch.topk`).
- `crates/cuda/csrc/dsa_*.cu`: the structure of the sparse selection (softmax-gated pool keys with a learned in-pool embedding, ReLU-weighted pool scores,
  512 selected pools of 4 tokens plus the raw tail, lowest-index ties, a row's attended tokens ascending with a count, dense rows per row) follows
  `src/tensorfold/families/glm5_next/cuda/sparse.py`; the numerics (fp16 planes and scores) follow the ExLlamaV3 fork instead, and the kernels are a CUDA rewrite.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/kda.cu`: the delta-rule `update()` (per-key-channel decay, read with k, correct toward v, write k times delta; warp owns
  value rows, lane owns 4 keys) and the idea of one update routine shared by the window and the replay are ported from
  `src/tensorfold/families/glm5_next/cuda/kda.cu`. The prep (conv, silu, l2norm, gates), the double-buffered state with a device-side
  parity, the input-record commit, the GEMVs and the gated norm are REXL3's own, written against the ExLlamaV3 fork's decode graph
  (MIT, read as an oracle: no code copied). `crates/cuda/src/kda.rs`, `crates/cuda/tests/kda.rs`, `tools/dump_kda_reference.py` and
  `tools/mutation_check_kda.py` are REXL3's own.
- `crates/cuda/csrc/mhc.cu`: the fixed K split of the mixing dots (`HC_BLOCKS`) and the stream mean follow `_hc_partial`, `_hc_finish`
  and `_stream_mean` in `src/tensorfold/families/glm5_next/cuda/glue.py`. TensorFold carries the streams in bf16; REXL3 carries them in fp32
  like the ExLlamaV3 oracle, so the rounding points differ.
- Tree drafting (`crates/cuda/src/tree.rs`, the `tree_*` kernels of `crates/cuda/csrc/gdn.cu` and `attention.cu`, `tools/tree_ceiling.py`): the
  structure follows TensorFold's tree verify as it stands in the MIT-era revision (the tree attention's split into committed chunks and a
  path-indirected tail, `PATHS` of root-to-row window rows, logical position `L + depth`; the GDN `tree_kernel`'s plan of `(node, source, dest)`
  entries and slot stack, and `replay_kernel`'s row-list `Pending`; the host `schedule()`). All of it is rewritten (a single attention kernel
  with a shared phase and a per-row phase over REXL3's own chunk/tile arithmetic, a depth-first plan with the heaviest subtree last, shared-memory
  slots), no code copied. `tools/tree_ceiling.py` reimplements `best_first` from `src/tensorfold/families/qwen3_5/cuda/draft_tree.py` (heap over
  summed sibling log-probabilities, `branch` best children per popped node, the edge / keyed-noise / scale constants) and `allocate`'s idea of
  expected tokens per millisecond; that file is identical at 0.6.2 and in the MIT-era revision. Nothing from the Apache-2.0 part of 0.6.x is used.
  The Rust `best_first` (`crates/spec/src/tree.rs`), `gb_sched::plan_trees` and the engine's tree round are rewrites of those two ideas in
  REXL3's own types (a lattice of the REXL3 selector, calibrated node probabilities from `measured_tree.rs`, a nested greedy allocation
  over the B6 cost grid); the constants (edge 0.6, keyed noise 0.7, scale 1.5, branch 4) are TensorFold's, chosen because they won our own
  24-point grid (docs/TREE_DRAFTING.md section 7). The copy branch (`CopyDrafter::propose_after`, `tree::with_copy`) is REXL3's own.
- `docs/CUDA_BACKEND.md`, `docs/PLAN.md` and `docs/UPSTREAMS.md` cite TensorFold's files and reported measurements.

## Image preprocessing (`crates/vision`)

- The preprocessing pipeline (convert to RGB over white, `qwen2_smart_resize`, float32 normalization and the block-major patch
  layout) reproduces ExLlamaV3's `exllamav3/architecture/qwen3_vl.py` `preprocess` and `mm_processing/{common,qwen2}.py`
  (MIT, turboderp; the fork at `r0b0tlab`). The patch layout and rounding are checked against those functions by running them
  (`tools/gen_vision_fixtures.py`).
- `crates/vision/src/resize.rs` reimplements the algorithm of Pillow's `libImaging/Resample.c` (bicubic filter, coefficient
  normalization, 22-bit fixed point, two-pass resample) so that results are byte-identical to `PIL.Image.resize(BICUBIC)`;
  Pillow is distributed under the HPND license (MIT-CVS style, permissive). No Pillow source is included.
- Runtime dependencies: `png` and `zune-jpeg` (MIT / Apache-2.0 / Zlib).
- GLM-5.3-Flash preprocessing (`crates/vision/src/{glm,resample,rgb_decode}.rs`) reproduces HuggingFace Transformers' `Glm5NextImageProcessor`
  (Apache-2.0: the canvas rule `smart_resize`, the content scale, the zero pad, `patchify`; the algorithms are re-expressed in Rust, no source is
  included) and the ExLlamaV3 fork's `Glm5NextVisionModel.preprocess` (MIT, as above). Both are run by `tools/gen_glm_vision_fixtures.py`, which
  loads them at run time and copies no code. That script also runs torch and torchvision (the HF processor's resize and normalisation) as
  oracles to record their outputs: torchvision's code is run, not ported; the one piece of PyTorch that is ported is the resize kernel in the next bullet.
  `Profile::Fork` resizes with the Pillow algorithm above (`crates/vision/src/resample.rs` only adds Pillow 12.2's two-step rule for very tall images
  to `resize.rs`); `Profile::Hf` resizes with PyTorch's kernel (next bullet). The JPEG decoder is the `zune-jpeg` dependency; nothing of
  libjpeg-turbo / IJG is ported.
- WebP: the `image-webp` crate 0.2.4 (image-rs, https://github.com/image-rs/image-webp, MIT OR Apache-2.0, used under MIT; pure safe
  Rust) decodes the pixels, with its two dependencies `byteorder-lite` 0.1.0 (Unlicense OR MIT, used under MIT) and `quick-error` 2.0.1
  (MIT OR Apache-2.0, used under MIT). Their licence files are reproduced in `docs/VISION_INGRESS_LICENSE_TEXTS.txt`. The container
  checks and the alpha-plane filters of `crates/vision/src/webp.rs` follow the WebP container specification and the behaviour of libwebp's
  `alpha_dec.c` / `filters.c` (BSD-3-Clause, Google), observed by decoding test files with Pillow; no libwebp source is included or ported.
- `crates/vision/src/torch_resample.rs` re-expresses the weight and precision logic of PyTorch's `aten/src/ATen/native/cpu/UpSampleKernel.cpp`
  (`_compute_index_ranges_int16_weights`, `_compute_indices_min_size_weights_aa`, the uint8 separable loops) and the `cubic_convolution1/2` of
  `aten/src/ATen/native/UpSample.h`, so that uint8 antialiased bicubic resizing is byte-identical to `torchvision.transforms.v2.functional.resize`
  (what the HF torchvision image processors run), and runs its rows on threads. PyTorch is BSD-3-Clause, not MIT or Apache-2.0; its license text
  follows, as it requires.
- `crates/vision/src/nemotron_video.rs` (the Nemotron video path's EVS pruning, `docs/NEMOTRON_VIDEO.md`) re-expresses two more files of
  PyTorch, both under `aten/src/ATen/native/cpu/`: `SumKernel.cpp` (`vectorized_inner_sum`, `row_sum` and `multi_row_sum`: the order in
  which a bf16 `sum` over the last dimension adds its terms) and `ReduceOpsKernel.cpp` (the 2-norm's last-dimension path), so that the host's
  `cosine_similarity` scores, and with them the retention mask, are bit-identical to `torch.nn.functional.cosine_similarity` on a CPU. Licence
  checked 2026-10-11 against the upstream files: neither carries a licence or copyright header of its own, so both are under the PyTorch
  repository's top-level `LICENSE`, the BSD 3-Clause-style text reproduced below (copyright lines of Facebook, Inc. and the other holders it

## Rust dependencies (crates.io)

The binaries link crates from crates.io, each under its own licence (the licence ids named by the manifests of the linked crates: MIT, Apache-2.0, Unicode-3.0, ISC, Zlib, Unlicense, BSD-3-Clause, 0BSD, BSD-2-Clause, BSL-1.0, CDLA-Permissive-2.0). Their licence and notice files, verbatim from the checksum-verified package archive of every package in `Cargo.lock`, are in `sbom/DEPENDENCY_NOTICES.md`; the inventory (version, SPDX expression, archive sha256) is `sbom/cargo.cdx.json`. A distributed binary or image carries `sbom/DEPENDENCY_NOTICES.md` with it. `tools/dependency_notices.py` regenerates both from `Cargo.lock`, and CI checks that the committed copies match.
